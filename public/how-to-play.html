How to Play

This guide walks you through your first season as general manager, from picking a team to your first offseason. Everything here applies to both random leagues and real NBA history leagues.

1. Start a league

  1. On the main menu, choose Random Players (a freshly generated 30-team league) or Real League (real NBA history from any season since 1946-47).
  2. Pick a trade difficulty. Normal means trades need roughly matched value; Hard puts a premium on young high-potential players.
  3. Choose a team to run, or create an expansion team through a real expansion draft where every club files a protection list first.
  4. Pick Simple navigation for a guided sidebar, or Full to see every page. You can switch any time.

2. Set your rotation

Open Roster to order your depth chart: the first five are starters. Minutes targets decide who plays how much. Players who get fewer minutes than they expect lose morale and some ask to be traded, so give your best players the minutes they have earned.

Your head coach's system (pace, three-point frequency, defensive scheme) shapes how the team plays. The Coaching page lets you adjust tendencies; the coaching staff also affects player development.

3. Play the season

Use the Play button to simulate a day, a week, to the next event or the rest of the season. The season stops for the big moments:

  • In-Season Cup: group games, a knockout bracket and a final you can watch.
  • All-Star Weekend: captains draft their teams, plus the three-point contest, dunk contest and Rising Stars.
  • Trade Deadline Day: a 9 AM to 3 PM clock, a rumor mill, AI teams calling with offers, and deals breaking all day.
  • Playoffs: a play-in, four rounds, and your series live if you want to watch.

4. Make moves

  • Trades. Build an offer and the AI front office answers, often with a counteroffer instead of a flat no. Three-team trades are possible too.
  • Free agency. Players weigh money, role, winning and loyalty. Negotiate through their agents; a player who walks away will not sign with you that offseason.
  • Contracts. Extend players during the season before they reach free agency. Watch the salary cap and the luxury tax on the Finances page.
  • Medical room. When a player is hurt, the doctors ask for a call: rest him longer or bring him back early at a risk.

5. The offseason

After the Finals come the awards, the Draft Lottery and Draft Night, where you can trade picks in the war room. Summer League follows, then re-signings, then free agency. Players age and develop over the summer: young players with minutes and good coaching grow the most. Your owner reviews your season against the goals set before it, and your job security depends on it.

Tips for your first season

  • Check Power Rankings and Standings before trading: know whether you are a contender or a seller.
  • A young player's Potential is his ceiling; minutes and coaching decide how close he gets.
  • Use Auto Play to simulate several seasons at once when you want to see a rebuild through.
  • Back up your progress now and then from the main menu (Backups) or the GM Locker.

The All-Time Draft

Pick All-Time Draft on the main menu, choose your franchise, your draft slot (or a random one) and the era whose rules the season is played under. Thirty teams then draft in snake order for thirteen rounds from every player in NBA history, each at his best season. The table shows every player's rating, best season and, for the all-time greats, their place in the all-time Top 100; filter by guards, forwards or bigs, or search a name.

AI GMs take the best player left for what their roster still needs, so bigs and guards run out as the rounds go on. Best fit suggests your pick, and Auto-draft the rest finishes for you. When the draft is done you see where your team ranks on paper; then the season starts in a normal GM league: trades, free agency, the draft, and as many seasons as you like, with players aging and developing from their best years.
