Career Mode guide

In Career Mode you create one player and live his whole career, season by season, in a real league played by the full simulation engine. At the end you see where he ranks among the greatest players of all time.

Choose when he enters the league

Start in today's league (the 2026 draft) or pick any past draft from 1947 to 2025. In a past draft the league is real NBA history from the season before: real rosters, real players on their real careers, and the real draft class. Enter the 1984 draft and you share the stage with Michael Jordan, Hakeem Olajuwon and Charles Barkley.

Random mode: the wheel

Every real player in NBA history sits on the wheel once, at his best season. The odds lean a little toward good players: a Star comes up about 7% of the time and a Great about 18%. When the wheel stops you take one of that player's ten categories, and your player gets his exact ratings in it.

Tools: Move Left and Move Right shift a stopped wheel by one slice (once each), two Respins spin again without taking anything, and one Triple Spin spins three wheels at once so you can take a category from each.

The best ever at a skill go past 99. The top 60 in each skill, ranked by what they actually did that season (threes made, assists, steals, rebounds and blocks, efficient scoring), carry up to +21, so the very best reach 120. Take Stephen Curry's shooting and your player shoots like the best shooter ever. On the court, points above 99 count half, so a 120 shooter is the greatest ever, not a 70% three-point shooter.

MyPlayer: build him yourself

Set height, weight and wingspan, then spend a budget of points across nine skills. Height sets the limits: a seven-footer cannot handle like a point guard and a small guard cannot protect the rim like a center. You also choose how ready he is: a late bloomer starts further from his prime but peaks higher; an NBA-ready rookie starts close to it.

Every season

  • Draft night. Where he goes depends on his rating and potential against the rest of the class.
  • Training focus. Each summer pick two categories. They grow faster, and training can carry them past his prime. Everything grows quickly when he is young and declines with age, athleticism first.
  • Free agency. When his contract runs out he gets offers: the biggest contract, a title contender, the biggest role, or his old team.
  • Trade requests. Ask out to a contender, for a bigger role, or anywhere but here.
  • Autopilot. Let the game make sensible choices for the rest of his career; take control back whenever you like.

Each season shows his stats, his team's record and playoff run, every award, and the moments of his year: his first 40-point game, his first triple-double, 20,000 career points.

Legacy, the Hall of Fame and the Top 100

When he retires you get his full career: totals, per-game averages, a pixel trophy shelf, his Overall by age, and any team that retires his number (five seasons there plus three All-Star years, a title or an MVP). His Legacy Score decides the Hall of Fame (45 gets him in, 90 makes him a first-ballot pick) and his place on the all-time Top 100. The Ratings explained guide shows how the score is built.

Your Hall of Famers get plaques in the Career Mode Hall of Fame and in the GM Locker. A retired or active player can also be brought into any GM league from the Free Agency page.
