Ratings explained

Court Vision uses a few different numbers. This page explains what each one means and how it is built.

On this page
  1. Player Overall
  2. Team rating, 0 to 100
  3. Ratings past 99
  4. Legacy Score
  5. The all-time Top 100

Player Overall

Overall is a weighted summary of a player's most important ratings for his position: shooting, finishing, playmaking, defense, rebounding and athleticism. It is a quick guide, not the whole story: the engine uses every individual rating and tendency, so two 75-rated players can play very differently.

Team rating, 0 to 100

League Hunt rates teams from their best six players, weighted from the top (25%, 21%, 18%, 15%, 12%, 9%), because one great player is worth more than two good ones. That weighted strength is lined up against the real distribution of win totals across every team-season in NBA history, then turned into a rating:

RatingReal-world meaning
60a bad team, about 25 wins
70about 40 wins
80about 45 wins
90a 55 to 62 win team
100a 68-win all-time great

No real team goes past 100, but a squad built in League Hunt can.

Ratings past 99

In Career Mode, the top 60 players ever at a skill carry ratings past 99, up to 120, ranked by what they really did: threes made (Stephen Curry), assists (John Stockton, Steve Nash), steals, rebounds and blocks (Wilt Chamberlain, Dikembe Mutombo). In games, every point past 99 counts half.

Legacy Score

The Legacy Score measures a whole career. It is computed the same way for real players (from the NBA history data) and for players you create:

AchievementPoints
MVP10 each
Finals MVP6 each
Championship5 each
All-NBA First / Second / Third Team5 / 3 / 1.5 each
Defensive Player of the Year3 each
All-Star selection1.5 each
All-Defensive First / Second Team1.2 / 0.6 each
Rookie of the Year1
Career points / rebounds / assists1.6 / 0.9 / 1.2 per 1,000
Career steals + blocks0.8 per 1,000

A Legacy Score of 45 puts a player in the Hall of Fame; 90 makes him a first-ballot pick.

The all-time Top 100

The Top 100 is a fixed, hand-ordered list: LeBron James is 1, Michael Jordan 2, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar 3, Tim Duncan 4, Stephen Curry 7 and Kobe Bryant 12. A created player's place on it is one plus the number of listed players whose Legacy Score is higher than his.
