Your Career guide

Your Career follows one player from a youth club at 13 to his last game. You build him, then every season brings choices: which school, stay or transfer, when to declare for the draft, which contract, how to train. The seasons in between play themselves.

Create your player

  • Choose a name, number, country, position and height. Each position has its own height range and weighs ratings differently in your Overall: point guards lean on vision and handles, centers on physical play and defense.
  • Pick a background. City courts start with more reputation and handles; a small town player starts unknown with more room to grow; a hoops family brings good habits and a famous name to live up to.
  • Answer eight playing-style questions, from how you like to score to what your summer workouts look like. Each answer moves your starting ratings, and leaning hard one way costs you a little elsewhere.
  • Your five ratings are shooting, dribbling, vision, defense and physical. Your strongest two give you an archetype, such as Floor General, Slasher or Rim Protector.

Each level is tougher than the last, so a player who dominated in high school can find college much harder. After high school, recruiting offers decide where you go next.

Declaring for the draft

At the end of each college year you can declare, come back for another year, enter the transfer portal, or test the waters and keep your eligibility. Scouts give a projection from Top 3 pick down to likely undrafted. It depends mostly on your Overall, then your reputation and your age, because teams pay for upside. After four college years, or out of an academy, you must go pro.

Contracts and free agency

When a contract runs out, the phone rings. NBA players can re-sign with a loyalty bonus. Other offers come in three kinds: a contender with title odds but maybe a bench role, a rebuilding team offering max money on a shorter deal, or a team that wants you to be the guy. From age 33 you can also retire.

Growth and aging

Young players improve fastest: about three and a half points a season in the early teens, slowing through the early twenties, and nearly flat by 28. From 30, ratings start to fall, faster every year. Each season you choose a training focus, and between seasons story events ask you to decide what to do when things go right or wrong.

Your career ends when you retire, at 41 at the latest, or earlier if your Overall falls too low after 35.

Awards and Legacy

  • Every level has its own trophies: youth national titles, State champion and All-State, college honors, and in the NBA Rookie of the Year, All-Star, All-NBA, MVP, Defensive Player of the Year, Sixth Man of the Year, championships and Finals MVP.
  • Your Legacy score counts NBA seasons only: how far your Overall rose above 70, your scoring, and your trophies, with an MVP worth the most.
  • A Legacy of 160 or more puts you in the Hall of Fame.

Tips

  • Pick a position that fits your height range: a 6-foot center is a long road.
  • Staying in college another year can raise your draft stock, but the projection rewards youth, so do not wait too long.
  • For an all-time career in the classic GM game, try Career Mode instead.
