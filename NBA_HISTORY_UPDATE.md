# NBA history for Real League

> **Update — facts only.** Teams now carry city names ("Golden State", "Seattle"; the Clippers are "LA"), and players can type their own names in League Settings → League Rules → NBA history → Team names. No 2K or other game ratings are used anywhere: every rating is Court Vision's own, computed from the statistics. The data sources are credited in the menu, League Settings and the NBA History page. Leagues made with the first version are converted on load (see "Saves"). Ads now stay hidden until Google actually sends one (see `ADS_LOADING_FIX.md`).

Main menu → **Real League** → source **NBA history** → pick a start season (1946–47 to 2025–26) → **Real Player Development** (on by default) → pick a team. The CSV import is still the other Real League source.

Starting in 2016 opens an unplayed 2016–17 season. Everything from 1946–47 to 2015–16 is imported history. From 2016–17 on, this league's own results are the history: Curry wins the 2017 title only if your simulation says so.

## Creating a historical league

- **Teams:** the NBA teams of the chosen season under city names, with franchise continuity. Seattle's seasons belong to Oklahoma City, New Jersey's to Brooklyn, and the Charlotte Hornets' history to today's Charlotte. When two teams share a city, the Clippers are "LA" and the 1976–77 Nets "New York (NYN)". Conferences follow today's alignment, which is exact from 2004–05. Players can rename teams (one by one, or by pasting `GSW = Name` lines); names are saved with the league only and past seasons keep their city names.
- **Rosters:** each player joins his first team of that season, top 15 by minutes. Everyone else who played that season, plus established players who were absent that year but returned later, starts as a free agent. These are reconstructions, not verified opening-night rosters, and the game says so.
- **Kept out until their real debut:** retired players and future draftees are not in the league.
  - The next ten real draft classes are stored; the game loads more as seasons pass and before long Auto Play runs.
  - Players drafted earlier who debuted later (e.g. Bogdan Bogdanović, 2014 pick, debuted 2017–18) arrive as free agents in their debut season.
  - After the last real draft (2025), classes are generated fictional players, and a note says so.
- **Simulated components:** contracts, coaches, budgets, schedules and rules are generated by Court Vision and labelled in League Settings → League Rules → NBA history → Data notes. Season length follows the real season (61 games for 1946–47, 82 in the modern era).

## Players, statistics and ratings

- **Stable IDs:** every real player keeps his Basketball-Reference id (e.g. `curryst01`). Same-name players get a debut year, e.g. "Bill Smith (1972)".
- **Imported seasons:**
  - A multi-team season counts once (the "TOT" line), with a row per team underneath. Seattle-era seasons show "SEA".
  - Stats an era did not record show "—", never 0: steals and blocks before 1973–74, turnovers before 1977–78, 3-pointers before 1979–80, minutes before 1951–52, and so on.
  - Career totals add up only the seasons that recorded a stat and are marked "*". Per-game averages divide by those seasons' games.
- **Ratings (Court Vision's own, statistics only):**
  - Each season, players are ranked by value over replacement per game: box plus/minus from 1973–74, estimated from PER and WS/48 for 1951–52 to 1972–73, and from win shares, points and assists before that. Both estimates are fitted on later seasons (r = 0.90 and 0.93).
  - The Nth-best player, scaled to a 30-team league, gets the Nth-best Overall of a generated Court Vision league, so a historical league has the talent spread the game engine is tuned for.
  - A rating for a season comes from the season before; debuts use their own season, and an injury-shortened season leans on earlier form.
  - Check against real honours: MVPs rank #2 (median) going into the next season and 82% are in the top five; All-NBA First Team players rank #4. Examples going into 2016–17: Curry 91, Durant 88, LeBron 86, Kawhi 84.
  - Box scores favour scorers and playmakers, so defence-first players (Rodman, Ben Wallace) and pre-1973–74 seasons are less certain. Profiles label every rating "From stats", "Interpolated" or "Estimated" (players who never played an NBA game).
  - Skill attributes are always estimated from each player's real rates and labelled that way. Height, weight and age are real.
- **Rookie eligibility** uses prior seasons played: a player with imported seasons is not a rookie.

## Real Player Development

The setting is chosen at league creation and can be changed in **League Settings → League Rules → NBA history**. It is saved with the league.

- **On:** real players' ratings follow their real season-by-season reference, applied once at each season rollover.
  - Applying it again for a season, loading a save or opening a page never re-applies development or ages a player twice.
  - Training, coaching, facilities, minutes, offseason camp, projects and the sandbox "development pass" cannot change a real player's ratings or positions.
  - Practice still affects workload, recovery, morale, system familiarity and role familiarity. The Development page and each player's Development tab explain this.
  - A real player who really played the coming season skips random retirement.
- **Gaps and endings:**
  - A season between two reference points (an injury year, a retire-and-return) is interpolated and labelled **Interpolated**.
  - Before a player's first reference point, and after his last, Court Vision's normal development, aging and retirement take over, and the profile says from which season. Nothing is extrapolated, so nobody is immortal.
- **Off:** real players develop exactly like everyone else, starting from their current ratings. Nothing is reset.
- **Switched back on:** each real player resumes his reference at the next rollover, and his profile says so. Stats, awards and records are untouched.
- **Consistency:** manual progression and Auto Play run the same rollover code.
- **Fictional players** (generated classes, custom players) are unaffected.

## Awards and history

- **Imported before the start:**
  - Champions and series results.
  - MVP, Finals MVP, DPOY, ROY, MIP, Sixth Man and Clutch Player, with real voting shares and first-place votes.
  - All-NBA/BAA, All-Defensive and All-Rookie teams with rank.
  - All-Star selections, including injury replacements, and All-Star Game MVP.
  - ABA MVP, ABA ROY and ABA All-Star selections on player profiles.
  - Shared awards keep both winners (for example, the 1994–95 co-Rookies of the Year, Grant Hill and Jason Kidd).
- **Coach of the Year** goes into league and coach history, never onto a player.
- **Championship credit:** a player is credited when his *last* regular-season team won, so a player traded away mid-season does not get the title. Player playoff rosters are not in the data; this is stated wherever titles are shown.
- **Player profile awards:** two groups, **Real NBA · before 2016–17** (Imported) and **In this league** (Simulated).
- **League pages:**
  - The Almanac's imported seasons show real vote shares ("real voting") and the real standings.
  - Team History lists the franchise's real seasons under the city at the time (e.g. "1978 Seattle · Champion"), and its banners include real titles.
  - Records use the imported seasons of players in the league but never count a season that did not record the stat.
- **NBA History page (new, League menu):**
  - Players: search, year range, league/retired filter, sort, 50 per page. Clicking a player opens his full season-by-season line with team stints and honours. Kobe Bryant, for example, shows 20 seasons, 1,346 games and 33,643 points.
  - Teams: standings by season, with a champion banner.
  - Champions: with a team filter.
  - Awards: every category, with a year range and name filter.
  - Coverage: sources, licences, gaps and how ratings are made.
  - It shows nothing at or after the league's start, so future real results stay hidden.

## Saves

- A historical league stores:
  - its start year and dataset version;
  - the Real Player Development switch;
  - each real player's id and reference trajectory, which is hidden from the UI;
  - the upcoming real draft classes and pending debuts;
  - the imported careers of players in the league;
  - the league's imported history.
- Old saves and random/CSV leagues are unchanged, because every new field is optional.
- Leagues created with the first version (`nba-history.v1`) are converted once when loaded or imported: reference ratings move to Court Vision's scale (labelled "legacy"), and team names in the league and its history become city names. Free-text logs such as news items are not rewritten.
- JSON exports turn "not recorded" numbers into `null`. Import turns them back, so an exported and re-imported league still shows "—".
- A historical league starts at about 6 MB (a 30-team random league is about 1.5 MB) and grows by about 9.5 MB per season (random: about 7.5 MB), mostly from game logs and training history, as before.

## Not included (explicit gaps)

- Player **playoff statistics** (not in the source); imported seasons have no playoff lines.
- **Opening-night rosters** (reconstructed as described above).
- Game or publisher ratings of any kind (deliberately; all ratings are computed from statistics).
- Official team names (deliberately; players can type their own).
- Conference Finals MVP, sportsmanship, teammate, hustle and citizenship awards, weekly/monthly awards, contest winners and stat titles. The source files used do not contain them.
- Game highs, double-double counts and shot locations for imported seasons.
- Draft rights are not modelled for late debuts; those players arrive as free agents.
- Team-by-team rosters of imported seasons are in the NBA History archive, not in Team History.
- Jersey numbers are not in the data; they are generated.
